# Investable Company Gaps in Ethiopia (Research Brief, Nov 2026)

**Approach:** each business below already works well in a developed market but is missing or very early in Ethiopia. Every gap is backed by 2025–2026 data, and the ideas are ranked by how ready investors are to fund them.

---

## Why now: the 2024–2026 changes that make these ideas possible

| Change | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| **Banking open to foreign banks** (Proclamation 1360/2024; two foreign banks applied for new licences in Oct 2026) | More competition, and banks will look for fintech partners |
| **Market-based exchange rate + FX forwards** (Feb 2026 directive) | Investors can price currency risk and get money out more easily |
| **Ethiopian Securities Exchange** (Jan 2025); first foreign investment-banking licence (June 2026) | Gives investors a possible exit, so far a small one |
| **Startup Proclamation No. 1396/2026** | Angels, VCs and accelerators now have legal standing |
| **Credit-bureau upgrade with IFC**, full compliance due Dec 2026 | Lending based on data, not collateral, becomes possible |
| **Telebirr ~59M users, M-Pesa ~5.2M active**, linked through EthSwitch | Payment rails exist, but only ~15% of 140M mobile-money accounts are active |
| **Ban on importing petrol/diesel cars** | A whole EV market is being forced into existence |

**Risks investors will ask about:** very little local VC money, few exits, high capital thresholds for foreign investors, FX shortages, red tape (policy says one thing, practice another), and weak AI/digital readiness (World Bank puts Ethiopia in the bottom quartile).

## The ranked opportunities

### 1. Merchant software + data-based SME lending
**Developed-market models:** Square / Toast (merchant software) + Kabbage / Shopify Capital (lending based on sales data)

- **Gap:** ~850,000 MSMEs, but only ~130,000 can get credit. The financing gap is ~**$4.2B**, and one 2026 study puts the business credit gap at **2.6 trillion birr**. Formal digital lenders supply **<5%** of merchant borrowing.
- **Key insight:** most merchants take digital payments into *personal* accounts, so lenders cannot see business transaction data. Whoever owns the merchant's daily software (sales, inventory, bookkeeping, accepting Telebirr/M-Pesa) owns the data to underwrite loans.
- **Model:** a cheap or free merchant app (point of sale, stock, invoices) → revenue from payment fees → loans to merchants funded by partner banks (you earn a fee, the bank holds the loan on its books).
- **Competition:** Kifiya's Qena/Michu and bank apps such as Awash do lending, but nobody owns the merchant's daily workflow.
- **Investor angle:** this is the same playbook as Moniepoint, which reached unicorn status in Nigeria. It is the clearest fintech story for Ethiopia.

### 2. Embedded / digital insurance (managing agent model)
**Developed-market models:** Lemonade, Root, Bolttech (insurance sold inside other apps)

- **Gap:** insurance penetration is **~0.3% of GDP**, lower than in 1991. Retail products are **<10% of premiums**, and most of the rest is compulsory motor insurance. Agriculture is 32% of GDP but has almost no insurance. Only two insurers offer microinsurance.
- **Model:** become a **managing general agent** that designs products and runs the tech while a licensed insurer carries the risk. Sell simple products (hospital cash, funeral, crop, phone, loan-protection) through Telebirr/M-Pesa, lenders, and digitised *Iddir* groups. A UNDP pilot already put 1M+ Iddir members on a platform, and 170k got life cover.
- **Investor angle:** fast-growing premiums from a tiny base, few capital needs if you are not the risk carrier, and a proven playbook from Kenya and India.

### 3. Coffee traceability + EUDR compliance platform (earns US dollars)
**Developed-market models:** Sourcemap, Farmers Business Network, Indigo Ag

- **Gap:** coffee is Ethiopia's biggest export (~$1.2–1.5B+ per year, record volumes in 2024/25). About 95% comes from smallholders. The **EU Deforestation Regulation** requires farm-level geolocation (plot polygons) for EU-bound coffee, with deadlines currently reported as **Dec 2026 / June 2027** (check against official EU sources).
- **Model:** a mobile app for farmer and plot mapping, plus a traceability ledger and EUDR due-diligence reports. Charge exporters a subscription per ton. Later, add a **direct-trade marketplace** that links traced specialty lots to roasters abroad, plus export pre-financing.
- **Competition:** foreign vendors (e.g., TraceX) and government systems. A local, well-built operator with field teams has an advantage.
- **Investor angle:** revenue in **USD/EUR**, which solves the FX problem investors fear most. The deadline creates urgent demand, and the model can extend to sesame, cocoa and other commodities.

### 4. Cold-chain-as-a-service
**Developed-market models:** Lineage Logistics, Americold (ColdHubs is the African proof)

- **Gap:** only about **1 in 10** Oromia coffee and horticulture farmers can reach cold storage (single industry source; verify), and rural cold chain is "underdeveloped." The government has a National Cool Logistics Network plan, and Ethio Telecom is building temperature monitoring.
- **Model:** solar-powered cold rooms at collection points and markets, rented **per crate per day**, plus refrigerated trucks and IoT monitoring. Later, add a produce marketplace.
- **Investor angle:** needs a lot of capital, so it fits **blended or infrastructure finance** (IFC, Proparco, impact funds) more than classic VC. Real assets and steady usage revenue.

### 5. EV charging network + EV/battery financing
**Developed-market models:** ChargePoint, Tesla Supercharger + fleet lenders (Zeno/BasiGo in Kenya)

- **Gap:** the car import ban is pushing people to EVs, but counts of public chargers range from **13 to fewer than 100 to ~500** depending on the source. Most are in Addis and are slow chargers. Banks have stopped financing conversion kits, and EV financing is scarce.
- **Model:** fast chargers at fuel stations, malls and taxi hubs; **battery-swap for motorbikes and three-wheelers (bajaj)**; pay-as-you-go EV loans for taxi and ride-hail drivers using telematics data.
- **Investor angle:** cheap hydropower, a market created by government policy, and climate-fund appetite. **Risk:** unreliable power supply, and the import ban could be reversed.

### 6. B2B pharma marketplace + e-prescription
**Developed-market models:** McKesson / Amazon Pharmacy (mPharma is the African proof)

- **Gap:** a fragmented supply chain of importer → wholesaler → pharmacy. **34%** of health spending is paid out of pocket. Community insurance (CBHI) is growing, but claims are on paper.
- **Model:** a B2B ordering platform for pharmacies with inventory credit, plus e-prescriptions and digital insurance claims.
- **Competition:** early players exist (Tenadam, MedRafa, Chemed), but none has clear scale yet. Execution and regulation matter more here than the idea.

### 7. Diaspora proptech: verified listings + mortgage marketplace
**Developed-market models:** Zillow + Rocket Mortgage + escrow/title services

- **Gap:** a large housing deficit, mortgages **<3%** of bank credit, and families build homes slowly using *Equb*, remittances and informal loans. Banks such as CBE and Wegagen offer diaspora loans with up to 80% loan-to-value, but finding and checking property is opaque and fraud-prone.
- **Model:** verified listings and developer projects, **escrow tied to construction milestones** (big diaspora pain point), and a marketplace for comparing mortgages that earns referral fees from banks.
- **Investor angle:** buyers pay in foreign currency, an IFC-backed housing-finance institution is planned, and trust is the product.

### 8. Retail investing / brokerage app
**Developed-market models:** Robinhood, Wealthfront

- **Gap:** ESX is new, and only ~11 capital-market service providers were licensed (Oct 2025). Banks must raise new equity (5B birr minimum capital by July 2026), which means share offerings that need retail buyers.
- **Model:** partner with or obtain a brokerage licence, then offer an app for shares, T-bills and fractional investing, funded via Telebirr/M-Pesa.
- **Caveat:** few listed companies so far. Revenue is a bet on how fast the market grows. Best as a second-stage company.

## Already crowded, so not a "missing" business
- **B2B FMCG distribution to kiosks/souqs:** Jemla, Shemach and ChipChip are already there. ChipChip raised from Renew Capital and Digital Africa/Proparco in Oct 2026.
- **Ride-hailing, food delivery, basic mobile wallets:** existing local players plus Telebirr and M-Pesa.

## Shortlist for an investor pitch
1. **Merchant software + SME lending (#1).** Largest market, clearest unicorn comparable.
2. **Coffee/EUDR traceability (#3).** Dollar revenue, hard deadline, can start lean.
3. **Embedded insurance managing agent (#2).** Low capital, sells through partners' existing channels.

A strong combined thesis: **#3 + #1**. Traceability data on farmers and cooperatives becomes the underwriting data for input and export financing.

## Investors active in Ethiopia (starting list)
Renew Capital, Digital Africa (Proparco), Addis Ababa Angels, IFC, FSD Ethiopia (grants/insurance innovation), UNDP Insurance Innovation Challenge Fund, Catalyst Fund (Jobtech), plus Safaricom/M-Pesa and Ethio Telecom as strategic partners.

## What to validate before raising
- Licensing path for each idea (NBE for lending and insurance; ECMA for brokerage) and the foreign-ownership limits.
- 20–30 customer interviews (merchants, exporters, insurers) and one paid pilot.
- Current EUDR deadlines and the Startup Proclamation 1396/2026 incentives (startup label, tax breaks).
- Any figure marked "single source" above.
