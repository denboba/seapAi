The Forward Deployed Engineer course: content pipeline blueprint

How we build a premium Udemy and YouTube course system that follows the Forward Deployed Engineer skill roadmap, with a repeatable, high-quality, affordable production pipeline. This is the document we start working from.

01Executive summary

We'll build an original course family that teaches the skills on the Forward Deployed Engineer (FDE) roadmap, sold on Udemy and fed by a YouTube channel. The product is the pipeline: one structured lesson script turns into a 16:9 course lesson, 9:16 Shorts, captions, a thumbnail and metadata, with automated quality checks.

The bet

Demand for FDEs is surging, and existing courses are short, generic or exam-prep. Our edge is production quality plus real engineering: animated, code-verified lessons, a full customer-engagement capstone, and a consistent brand across YouTube and Udemy.

Key recommendations

  1. Follow the roadmap and never republish it. roadmap.sh content is copyrighted (personal use only). We cover the same skill areas with our own curriculum, structure, wording and visuals, and link to roadmap.sh as a free companion resource.
  2. Human-led, AI-accelerated. Udemy flags fully AI-generated courses with little instructor input, and YouTube demonetizes template-based, repetitive "inauthentic" content. So the instructor's real voice (recorded, or a verified clone of their own voice), real judgment and real code are in every lesson.
  3. Ship a focused first course, not the whole roadmap: "AI Engineering for Forward Deployed Engineers" (≈8–10 h). It covers the highest-demand, least-served part of the roadmap. Foundations courses come later.
  4. Build the pipeline on Remotion (React video framework, free for individuals and companies of up to 3 people), with ElevenLabs voice plus word timestamps, VHS for terminal demos, and CI that runs every code sample.
  5. Lean budget: ≈ $50–150/month during production, mostly voice credits. No expensive SaaS video editors needed.
First 2 weeks, in one lineFix the voice setup, lock the brand name, create the repo and channel, build a pipeline MVP with 5 scene templates, ship 1 pilot lesson and 3 Shorts, and send Udemy's free test video for feedback.

02Opportunity and competition

Market signal

  • Demand: Indeed data cited by Business Insider shows US FDE postings rising from 643 (Apr 2025) to 5,330 (Apr 2026). Bloomberry reports +1,165% YoY across 1,000 postings, and Paraform reports 10× growth in 18 months. India grew ~19× in a year.
  • Pay: median base ≈ $174k (Bloomberry), median advertised salary > $188k vs ≈ $145k for a traditional SWE role (Lightcast). Palantir FDE median total comp ≈ $215k (Levels.fyi, May 2026).
  • Why: AI vendors (OpenAI, Anthropic, Palantir and many startups) need engineers who can deploy AI inside customer systems. That takes engineering plus discovery, integration and stakeholder skills, a mix few people are trained in.

03Rules we must design around

These constraints shape the whole pipeline. Breaking them would cost us reach, revenue or the right to publish.

roadmap.sh: follow it, don't publish it must

  • The roadmap's license allows personal use only. Publishing its text, images or files in any form needs the copyright holder's consent. Linking to roadmap.sh is allowed.
  • Our approach: use the roadmap privately as a coverage checklist (its ~100 topic areas, §5). We write our own curriculum, module structure, explanations, diagrams and examples, and never reproduce the roadmap diagram, its descriptions or its resource lists.
  • Don't use "roadmap.sh" in the course title or brand, or imply endorsement. A line like "pairs well with the free FDE roadmap at roadmap.sh" with a link is fine.
  • optional Contact the roadmap author about a partnership (e.g. being listed as a course resource). That's upside, not a requirement.

Udemy must

  • Minimums: ≥ 30 min of video, ≥ 5 lectures, ≥ 720p, 16:9, clear audio in both channels, in sync. Use Udemy's free test video review before full production.
  • AI: AI-assisted production is allowed, but Udemy warns against fully AI-generated courses with little instructor involvement, factual errors and low-quality AI audio or visuals. Disclose the AI tools used in the course description. Udemy Business inclusion needs content that has been manually reviewed and polished.
  • Revenue: marketplace sales without instructor promotion use a single blended rate (it replaced separate organic and ads rates; check the current %). Instructor-coupon sales keep ~97%. The subscription pool share dropped to 15% in Jan 2026. So our own funnel (YouTube → coupon) is worth far more per sale.

YouTube must

  • The "inauthentic content" monetization policy (clarified July 2025, expanded July 2026) targets mass-produced, repetitive, template-based videos with little variation, whether or not AI was used. Reviews weigh the channel as a whole.
  • Our approach: every video has a distinct teaching idea, the instructor's own voice and opinions, original diagrams and real code. Templates give us consistency, but no two videos share the same script shape. Disclose realistic synthetic media where YouTube requires it.
Design consequenceThe pipeline accelerates a human instructor; it doesn't replace one. The instructor writes or approves every script, records or clones their own voice, and signs off each lesson. That's also what makes it good.

07The production pipeline

One source of truth per lesson: a structured lesson spec. Everything else is generated from it, then checked by machines and by a human.

08Quality bar: definition of done

Automated gates (CI blocks the render)

  • All lesson code passes tests; snippets match the tagged commit
  • Resolution ≥ 1080p, 30 fps, 16:9 (Shorts 9:16)
  • Loudness −14 LUFS ±1, true peak ≤ −1 dBTP; no clipping
  • Captions present, word timing drift < 100 ms
  • Spelling and terminology lint (glossary-enforced)
  • No text outside safe areas; contrast check on captions
  • Links in descriptions resolve

Human review (instructor signs off)

  • Technically correct; claims sourced; nothing outdated
  • One clear learning goal, met by the end
  • Pacing: something changes on screen at least every 5–8 s in explainers
  • The voice sounds natural; names pronounced correctly
  • Original: no copied text, diagrams or roadmap reproduction
  • Accessible: readable at phone size, colour-blind-safe meaning
  • Would I pay for this? If not, it doesn't ship.

Revenue mechanics (illustrative, not a forecast)

  • Udemy courses often sell at discounted prices of ~$10–20. Organic sales pay the instructor the blended marketplace rate (check the current %), while your own coupon links pay ~97%.
  • That's why YouTube matters: every buyer we bring ourselves is worth roughly 2–3× an organic buyer.
  • At ≈ $150/month peak cost, break-even is a few dozen sales a month. The upside comes from the bundle, Udemy Business (needs high polish) and, later, our own platform.
